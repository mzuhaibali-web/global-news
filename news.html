IMF Global Growth Outlook for 2026: AI Investment Supports Growth as Risks Persist

Published: October 13, 2026

The outlook for the world economy depends on several connected factors, including investment in technology, energy costs, government debt and international conflicts. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has highlighted both the support provided by artificial intelligence investment and the risks that could slow economic growth.

In its October 13, 2026 report, Reuters reported that the IMF's latest World Economic Outlook projected global real gross domestic product (GDP) growth of 3.1% for 2026, compared with its previous estimate of 3.0%. The report identified strong investment in artificial intelligence as one factor supporting economic activity.

How AI Investment Can Support Economic Growth

Artificial intelligence is creating demand for computing equipment, data centres, chips, software and digital infrastructure. Businesses investing in these areas can generate orders for technology suppliers and related industries.

The effects may extend beyond technology companies. Businesses in finance, manufacturing, logistics and other sectors are exploring ways to use AI to improve productivity and automate certain tasks.

However, investment spending does not automatically guarantee lasting economic benefits. The long-term impact will depend on how effectively businesses use these technologies and whether the financial returns justify their costs.

Energy Prices and International Conflicts Remain Risks

The global economy also faces risks unrelated to technology. International conflicts can disrupt energy supplies, increase transport costs and make businesses less confident about investing.

Higher energy prices can raise production and delivery costs. Businesses may pass some of those increases on to consumers, contributing to inflation and reducing household purchasing power.

Countries that rely heavily on imported energy can be particularly exposed to sudden price changes. The effects differ between countries depending on their energy sources, trade relationships, public finances and policy responses.

Government Debt and Uneven Growth

Government debt is another important consideration. Countries with high borrowing costs may have less room to respond to economic shocks or invest in public services and infrastructure.

The benefits of the AI investment boom may also be distributed unevenly. Economies with access to skilled workers, reliable electricity, digital infrastructure and investment capital may be better positioned to benefit than countries that lack these resources.

What This Means for Businesses and Consumers

For businesses, the outlook makes cost management, careful investment decisions and productivity important. Companies may need to consider energy costs, borrowing conditions and the practical value of new technology before expanding their operations.

For consumers, changes in energy prices and inflation can affect household budgets. The effect on jobs, wages and prices will vary by country and industry. The global growth forecast alone cannot predict the economic experience of every household.

What to Watch Next

Future economic data, energy prices, central-bank decisions and the pace of AI adoption will help show whether the current growth outlook holds. IMF forecasts are estimates based on available information and can change as conditions develop.

Readers should consult the original report for the complete forecast, country-level estimates and explanations of the assumptions behind the projections.
