South Asia Economic Growth Outlook 2026: AI Opportunities and Regional Risks

By Global News Editorial Team

South Asia's economy is expected to grow by 6.9% in 2026, according to the World Bank's October 2026 regional outlook. The report says resilient domestic demand and remittance inflows have helped support activity despite external pressures. The Bank expects growth to moderate to 6.7% in 2027.

Key figures: South Asia regional growth is forecast at 6.9% for 2026 and 6.7% for 2027. These are regional estimates, not forecasts for every individual country.

Artificial intelligence and new opportunities

The World Bank's October 2026 South Asia Economic Update, Adopting AI for Growth, examines how AI could support productivity, expand export opportunities and improve public-service delivery. Firms and governments are beginning to use AI in areas such as business services, agriculture and healthcare.

These gains are not automatic. Businesses need reliable digital infrastructure, suitable skills, and the ability to integrate tools into real work. Public institutions also need appropriate safeguards for data and responsible use. For South Asia, a key challenge is making AI useful and accessible beyond a small group of large firms and highly skilled workers.

Energy prices and inflation risks

Many economies in the region are exposed to changes in imported energy prices. Persistently high oil and gas costs can raise transport, electricity and production expenses. That can squeeze household budgets, increase business costs and complicate efforts to bring inflation down.

Energy efficiency, reliable energy infrastructure and investment in a diverse mix of energy sources may help reduce some vulnerabilities over time. The effect of any measure depends on its cost, implementation and each country's circumstances.

Better water management, climate-resilient farming practices and effective early-warning systems can help communities prepare. These measures reduce some risks but cannot eliminate the uncertainty associated with extreme weather.

What readers should watch

  • Updates to regional and country-level economic forecasts.
  • Energy prices, inflation and household purchasing power.
  • Remittance flows, private investment, exports and job creation.
  • How businesses and public services adopt AI and build digital skills.
  • Agricultural output, rainfall patterns and climate-related disruptions.

Conclusion

The World Bank's 6.9% growth forecast suggests that South Asia is expected to maintain strong regional momentum in 2026, but the outlook differs by country and remains exposed to external shocks. AI may create opportunities for productivity and services, while energy costs and climate risks could weigh on households and businesses.

Following official forecasts and country-level indicators will give readers a clearer picture than relying on one regional figure alone. Forecasts are estimates and can change as new information becomes available. This article is for general information and is not investment advice.
