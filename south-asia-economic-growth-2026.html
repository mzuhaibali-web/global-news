South Asia Economic Growth Outlook for 2026

South Asia is expected to remain one of the fastest-growing emerging-market regions in 2026. Strong domestic demand has supported economic activity, although higher energy costs, climate risks and uncertainty in global markets remain important challenges.

Key outlook: The World Bank's October 2026 regional update expects South Asia's economy to grow by 6.9% in 2026, with growth projected to moderate in 2027. Forecasts can change as economic conditions evolve.

What is supporting economic growth?

Consumer spending, remittance inflows and domestic demand are helping support businesses across the region. Economic reforms and investment can also improve productivity when they make it easier for firms to expand, hire workers and reach new markets.

Artificial intelligence and new jobs

Artificial intelligence could help businesses improve productivity, reduce repetitive work and develop new services. Small businesses may benefit from practical AI tools in areas such as customer support, agriculture, healthcare and education.

These gains are not automatic. Countries need better digital infrastructure, workforce training, responsible data practices and clear policies so that more businesses and workers can benefit from new technology.

Energy prices and inflation risks

Many South Asian economies depend on imported energy. A prolonged rise in oil and gas prices can increase transport and production costs, put pressure on household budgets and make inflation harder to control. Investment in reliable energy supplies, including renewable power, can help improve resilience over time.

What to watch next

  • Changes in energy prices and consumer inflation.
  • Job creation and investment by private businesses.
  • AI adoption, digital skills and technology infrastructure.
  • Agricultural output and weather conditions.
  • Government reforms and regional trade opportunities.
